## 1. Competition rules (set by the course)

1. **Eligibility.** Only students enrolled in DSAI4203 may take part. The invitation link must stay inside the course. Entries whose account cannot be matched to an enrolled student are removed before marking. A pseudonymous Kaggle account is allowed, but each student must give their Kaggle display name in the checkpoint submission on Canvas, so that the entry can be identified.
2. **Teams.** Students compete alone or in a team of 2 or 3. The team is fixed at the checkpoint deadline and cannot change afterwards. A student may be in one team only and may hold only one Kaggle account for this competition; uploading through a second account, one's own or someone else's, is not allowed.
3. **Submissions.** At most 5 per day. The public leaderboard is computed on a small sample (2 %) of the test rows. The final standing comes from the private leaderboard, which uses the rest of the test rows and is shown only after the deadline; the public score is a guide during the competition, not the result. Files must follow the format of `sample_submission.csv`.
4. **External resources.** Any publicly available library, tutorial, documentation or published method may be used, and any published source may be consulted. Everything used must be cited in the report. On training data the rule says: "You may not use data from outside this competition to train your model."
5. **Sharing.** Code, data, predictions and results must not be shared privately with anyone outside the team, by any means; this includes students in other teams. Code and ideas posted openly on the competition's discussion forum may be used by everyone.
6. **Generative AI.** Allowed, but the report submitted on Canvas must disclose it: which tool, and what it was used for. With or without AI, participants must be able to explain every detail of their submission.
7. **How results are used.** The leaderboard position is a small part of the mark; details are in the project description on Canvas.
8. **Deadline.** The competition closes at 11:59 pm on Sunday 29 November 2026. Kaggle does not accept late submissions, and they cannot be accommodated afterwards. The rule gives no time zone (see §3).

## 2. Kaggle Foundational Rules: the parts that matter for us

Every participant must also accept Kaggle's Foundational Rules. They apply to every Kaggle competition; the course rules come on top of them, and where the two conflict the Foundational Rules win.

- **4b. No hand labelling.** A submission may not use, or contain, information from hand labelling or from human prediction of validation or test records.
- **5a. One account.** Each person submits from one Kaggle account only. Submitting from several accounts, or through a false account used as a proxy, leads to disqualification. The daily maximum is the one stated on the competition website.
- **5b. Teams.** Where teams are allowed, a person may join or form one team only. Each member is an individual with their own account, must first register for the competition individually, and joins officially only by confirming the team invitation sent to their Kaggle account. A team may not exceed the maximum size stated for the competition.
- **5c. Mergers.** Teams may request a merger on the website. It is allowed only if (i) the merged team stays within the maximum size, (ii) the merging teams together have not made more submissions than one team could have made by the date of the request, (iii) it is completed before any merger deadline and before the competition deadline, and (iv) the merged team meets all other rules.
- **5d. Private sharing.** No private sharing outside teams: code or data may not be shared privately with anyone outside the team. Sharing code is fine when it is made available to all participants on the forums.
- **6a. Private code sharing.** Unless the competition rules permit it, code developed with or based on the Competition Data, or otherwise relevant to the competition ("Competition Code"), may not be shared privately during the competition. This includes sharing between separate teams unless they merge. A breach can lead to disqualification.
- **6b. Public code sharing.** Competition Code may be shared publicly if it does not infringe anyone's intellectual property, but only on Kaggle (this competition's forum or notebooks), for all competitors. Code shared this way counts as licensed under an OSI-approved licence that never limits commercial use.
- **6c. Open-source code in the model.** Unless the competition rules say otherwise, any open-source code used in the model that generates a submission must carry an OSI-approved licence (opensource.org) that never limits commercial use of the code, or of a model that contains or depends on it.
- **7a. Ranking.** Each submission is scored with the competition metric (here ROC AUC). During the competition the public leaderboard (public test set) shows the current order; the final ranking is decided only by the private leaderboard (private test set), subject to the rules.
- **7b. Ties.** In a tie the submission entered first wins. If a potential winner is disqualified, the next-ranked submission takes its place.
- **18a. Competition Data.** The data offered on the competition website for use in the competition, including any code provided there. It contains the private and the public test sets; participants are not told which rows belong to which set.
- **18c. Final Submission.** The submission(s) the participant selects, or that Kaggle selects automatically if the participant selects none, used for the final leaderboard position.
- **18e, 18f, 18i.** The private leaderboard determines the final standing. The public leaderboard is computed on a representative sample of the test data and is visible throughout. A Team is one or more participants who have officially merged as a team on the platform.

## 4. What this means for us

1. **Test features are Competition Data (supports D007).** Rule 4 bans training on data from outside the competition. Under 18a the test file, public and private parts included, is Competition Data. Steps fitted on the train+test features therefore use competition data only: the label-free ones (scaling, the variance rule that picks the 13-column block, the two-component Gaussian mixture of S003) and the self-training of S006, which adds algorithmic pseudo-labels to test rows. Rule 4b bans information from hand labelling or human prediction of test records. Nobody labelled or predicted a test row by hand anywhere in this project: test scores, and S006's pseudo-labels, come only from algorithms. D010 item 6 also rules out any attempt to recover the hidden labels or the generator's seed, and our replica datasets (simulations, not competition data) never train a submitted model. D007 stays "accepted". The instructor has **not been asked yet** (no question is recorded as of 2026-11-01; corrected by D015, which also says the question must cover both uses: unsupervised fitting on the test features and S006's self-training on pseudo-labels). D010 keeps a strictly inductive second final (S005) plus a fallback if the answer is no. Basis recorded in D014.
2. **Team.** Two or three students, or one, fixed at the Canvas checkpoint deadline. Each member uses one Kaggle account and belongs to one team, and every member's Kaggle display name goes into the checkpoint submission (rule 1). A teammate counts as "inside the team" only after the Kaggle merger (18i): they join the competition, the team leader invites them on the Team tab, and they accept. Until then the repository, predictions and scores are not shared with them (rule 5, 5d). A merger now is within 5c: at most 3 members, and our 6 uploads are far below the allowance of one team.
